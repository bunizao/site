Conversation UI

A chat block for the end of a post. One fenced syntax, two or n speakers, per-speaker accent. Prototype — nothing here is wired into the blog yet.

@name …Declares a speaker. The flag me puts them on the right with a filled accent bubble. Keys: accent, avatar, label. The whole cast block is optional — undeclared speakers are auto-created on first use with a palette accent, and the first voice on stage becomes me.
avatar=…A URL or path (https://…, /img/x.png), a data: URI, a sprite id (#av-bot, rendered as <use> and tinted with the speaker's accent), or a literal emoji. Omit it and the speaker gets initials.
name: textOne bubble. Consecutive bubbles from the same speaker group automatically: the name sits inside the top bubble of the run, the avatar next to the last, and only the last squares off its corner.
  indentedAnother paragraph inside the same bubble. Two levels of grouping, because chats really do have both.
--- textCentred divider — a day break, a scene note, or nothing at all.
inline**bold**, *italic*, `code`, [text](url).

Two people

Drag the dashed corner to resize. Every breakpoint here is a container query, not a viewport query — so it survives being dropped into a 720px post, a sidebar, or a phone.

Group chat

Four voices. Names and avatars carry the accent; bubbles stay neutral — five coloured bubbles is a rainbow, not a conversation. Each name is walked toward the bubble until it clears WCAG AA, so no accent an author picks can render unreadable.

Avatars

Five forms, all through the one avatar= key: external URL, site sprite #id, data: SVG, a bare emoji, or nothing at all (initials). Raw inline <svg> markup is deliberately not a form — it would make a cast line unreadable, and data: covers it.

Length stress test

One character, one word, a Latin wall, a Chinese wall, mixed script, an unbreakable URL, inline code, and a three-paragraph bubble. Turn on guides to see the measure cap.
